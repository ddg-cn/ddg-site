# Sample Responsible Exit Policies

Three short policies to adapt: one for a **buyer** (brand, retailer, importer), one for a **factory**, and one for an **agent or trading company** that places orders on behalf of buyers. Each follows the same structure so the two sides of a relationship can see what the other has committed to. They are drawn from the [Responsible Exit Guide](./exit-guide) and from published policies and guidance: ACT (2022), C&A (2025), Fair Wear, FLA (2023), ETI with Shift (2026), the ABA and Responsible Contracting Project model clauses, and Chinese law.

Replace the text in [brackets]. Delete what does not apply. Have the final text checked against the law where you operate. A [role-based checklist](./exit-checklist) turns these commitments into steps.

## 1. Buyer policy

**[Company] Responsible Exit Policy**
*Approved by [body] on [date]. Owner: [function, e.g. Head of Sourcing]. Reviewed every [two] years.*

### 1.1 Purpose
We sometimes need to reduce or end orders with a supplier. When we do, we will leave in a way that does not shift the cost onto the supplier's workers. This policy sets out how.

### 1.2 Scope
This policy applies to:
- every decision to end, suspend or sharply reduce orders (by more than [30]% against the previous 12 months or the agreed forecast) with a supplier, its subcontractors, or a factory our agents use for our orders;
- moves of our orders by an agent or supplier to another factory;
- exits we are required to make by sanctions, import bans or other laws.

### 1.3 Principles
1. **Engagement before exit.** Where the reason is a labour, human-rights or environmental problem, we first use our leverage to fix it. We end the relationship only when that fails or is impossible, or when the law requires it.
2. **Notice in proportion to dependence.** The bigger our share of the supplier's output, the longer the notice and the more gradual the ramp-down.
3. **Pay what is owed first.** We pay for goods made and materials bought on our instructions. We do not use an exit to introduce new claims, discounts or penalties.
4. **Workers are told and protected.** We ask the supplier to inform and consult its workers' representatives, and we check that wages and severance are paid.
5. **We keep proof and stay reachable.** We keep records of each exit and keep our grievance channel open after the last order.

### 1.4 Commitments
| Our share of the supplier's output (last 12 months) | Minimum notice and ramp-down |
|---|---|
| Under 10% | One order cycle or season (about 3 months) |
| 10–30% | 6 months, with a written monthly volume forecast |
| Over 30%, or we are the sole or main buyer | 6–12 months, phased, with committed final orders and a check that severance can be paid |
| Exit because of severe abuse, or required by law | As short as the reason or the law requires, with every remaining step taken to reduce harm |

We will also:
- consider the supplier's peak season, Chinese New Year and other buyers leaving at the same time;
- offer to bring final payments forward if the supplier needs cash to pay severance;
- never cancel orders already in production without paying the costs incurred.

### 1.5 Procedure
1. **Decide.** The sourcing manager records the reason and evidence and runs an impact check (dependence, workers affected, money owed, social insurance arrears, timing, alternatives). Exits from suppliers where our share is over [30]%, or where the reason is a human-rights issue, are approved by [the responsible sourcing committee].
2. **Notify.** We send written notice with the reason, the timeline and a monthly volume forecast, and ask the supplier to share it with its union or worker representatives.
3. **Wind down.** We agree in writing the treatment of open orders, work in progress, materials, packaging, tooling, technical files and data.
4. **Pay and verify.** We pay on time. Where the exit leads to layoffs, we ask for the supplier's layoff plan, severance calculation and proof of payment.
5. **After.** Our grievance channel stays open to the supplier's workers for at least six months. If we learn workers were not paid, we act, by mediation, by contribution or by remedy.
6. **Forced exits.** Where sanctions or an import ban force an exit, Legal decides the date. We use whatever room the law leaves to pay for goods made, to give notice and to support workers, applying for licences where needed.

### 1.6 Transparency
Where we publish a supplier list, we update it when a supplier leaves it and show the date. We report the number of exits and how they were handled in our [annual sustainability or due-diligence report].

### 1.7 Responsibilities
| Who | Responsible for |
|---|---|
| [Head of Sourcing] | Applying this policy to every exit; training buyers |
| [Responsible sourcing / CSR] | Impact checks, worker-related follow-up, grievance channel |
| [Legal] | Contracts, sanctions and import-ban decisions, counter-measure risks, data |
| [Finance] | On-time payment; no new deductions at exit |

### 1.8 Records
For each exit we keep: the decision and reasons, the impact check, the notice, the forecast, the wind-down agreement, payment records, and evidence about workers (notice, consultation, severance) for [five] years.

## 2. Factory policy

**[Factory] Policy on Ending Customer Relationships, Closure and Relocation**
*Approved by [owner / board] on [date]. Owner: [General Manager]. Reviewed every [two] years.*

### 2.1 Purpose
Our workers should not pay for decisions they did not make. This policy covers how we end a relationship with a customer, and how we would close, relocate or reduce the workforce, in line with Chinese law and our customers' codes of conduct.

### 2.2 Scope
- Ending or not renewing a customer relationship.
- Reducing the workforce for economic reasons.
- Closing the factory, in whole or in part.
- Relocating within the city, to another province, or abroad.

### 2.3 Principles
1. **Follow the law's sequence.** Consult, pay, transfer, liquidate, deregister. No step is skipped.
2. **Wages and severance come first**, before other creditors, before shareholders, and before new investment elsewhere.
3. **Redeploy before dismissing.** We look for alternatives first: other lines, reduced overtime, training, transfer.
4. **Tell people early and in writing.** Workers, the union, customers, suppliers, the landlord and the authorities.
5. **Leave the site clean.** Waste, soil and permits are settled before we leave.

### 2.4 Commitments
- We keep a **severance reserve** of at least [three] months of total severance liability, reviewed every [six] months.
- We pay **social insurance and housing fund on full wages**.
- We give customers at least [the notice in the contract, and not less than 30 days'] written notice before ending supply, and finish lawful open orders where we can.
- We do not keep a customer's tooling or goods as security without a legal basis.
- We do not ask workers to resign "voluntarily" to avoid severance, and we do not put waivers of legal rights in any settlement.
- When we relocate, we offer transfer in writing with transport, housing and wage terms, and pay severance to those who do not move. A move abroad is treated as a closure of the Chinese employer.
- We do not start the same business elsewhere, in China or abroad, while workers at the old site are still owed money.

### 2.5 Procedure
1. **Decide.** The owner or board passes a written resolution with reasons, after hearing the union and workers (Company Law Art. 17). We run the impact check and calculate severance for every affected worker.
2. **Prepare.** We check social insurance and housing fund arrears and correct them. We list subsidies, tax incentives, land terms and sponsored programmes that must be repaid, completed or handed over. We commission the environmental close-out (hazardous waste, soil where required, permits, fees).
3. **Explain and consult.** Where 20 or more people, or 10% or more of staff, are affected, we explain to the union or all staff 30 days ahead, hear their views and report to the labour bureau (LCL Art. 41). Protected workers (Art. 42) are handled individually.
4. **Notify.** Customers, suppliers and the landlord receive written notice with the date and a contact.
5. **Pay.** Wages to the last day, shutdown pay, severance (Arts. 46–47), unused leave, and any arrears are paid on time and by bank transfer.
6. **Transfer.** Certificates, personnel files and social insurance are transferred within 15 days (Art. 50).
7. **Close.** Liquidation follows the legal order (Company Law Art. 236). We apply for deregistration only after wages are cleared (Decree 724 Art. 22).

### 2.6 Responsibilities
| Who | Responsible for |
|---|---|
| [Owner / General Manager] | Decision, funding the reserve, overall compliance |
| [HR] | Consultation, labour bureau report, severance, certificates and transfers |
| [Finance] | Reserve, payment records, liquidation order |
| [EHS] | Environmental close-out |
| [Sales] | Customer notice and the commercial wind-down |

### 2.7 Records and contact
We keep the resolution, consultation minutes, the labour bureau acknowledgement, severance calculations, payment records and environmental documents for at least [two years after closure, or longer where the law requires]. A named contact answers workers' and customers' questions for [six] months after the exit.

## 3. Agent or trading company policy

**[Company] Responsible Exit Policy for Order Placement**
*Approved by [director] on [date]. Reviewed every [two] years.*

### 3.1 Purpose
We place orders at factories on behalf of our customers. When we move orders away from a factory, the effect on its workers is the same as if the brand had left. This policy makes sure we and our customers handle that responsibly.

### 3.2 Scope
Every decision, ours or our customer's, to end, suspend or sharply reduce orders at a factory we use, including moving orders to another factory, another of our suppliers or another country.

### 3.3 Commitments
1. **Tell our customer.** Before moving a brand's orders away from a factory, we tell the brand, because under many brand policies (for example ACT) the move counts as the brand's exit.
2. **Apply the buyer's notice.** We give the factory notice in proportion to the share of its output our combined orders represent (see the table in section 1.4), unless our customer's policy requires longer.
3. **Pass payments through in full and on time.** We do not hold back payments due to the factory to cover our own claims at exit.
4. **Share what the factory needs to know.** Reasons, timeline and volume forecast, in writing, in Chinese where the factory is in China.
5. **Collect proof.** Where layoffs follow, we collect the factory's layoff plan and proof of payment and pass them to our customers.
6. **Keep a contact.** We name a contact for the factory and its workers for six months after the last order.

### 3.4 When our customer leaves
When a customer stops ordering through us, we tell the factories concerned as soon as we know, share the customer's timeline, and ask the customer to apply its exit policy. We do not hide a customer's exit from a factory.

### 3.5 Records
For each exit we keep the decision, the notices, the forecast, payment records and evidence about workers for [five] years, and share them with our customers on request.
