# Grievance Channels

**How workers raise problems, before, during and after a business relationship, and how to tell whether anyone listens**

*Short guide, edition 1, November 2026. For buyers, auditors and supplier factories, especially in China. Part of the series: stage 5 of the [business relationship lifecycle](/en/ddg#_0-8-where-each-guide-fits-the-life-of-a-business-relationship), and a thread through every other stage.*

::: tip In one minute
1. **A channel is only real if workers know it, trust it and see results.** A box on the wall and an empty log prove nothing.
2. **Zero complaints is a warning sign, not a pass.** When access improves, the number of complaints goes up first.
3. **In China the building blocks already exist:** enterprise mediation committees, unions, 12333, labour inspection and free arbitration. A good channel connects to them; it never asks workers to give them up.
4. **Buyers need a channel too.** Under EU and German law, companies must offer a complaints route that reaches their suppliers' workers, and keep it open after an exit.
5. **Verify, don't assume.** Use the checklist in section 6: trace real cases, ask workers, send a test message.
:::

## 1. Why channels fail

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| SOMO and GoodElectronics, 40 electronics factories in China and four other countries (2014) | Very few workers trusted company mechanisms; most did not know how complaints were handled; contract workers were often excluded |
| Amader Kotha helpline, Bangladesh (about 520,000 calls, 2014–2026) | Only **22%** of callers would give their name to the factory, against **85%** to the independent helpline |
| ETI study of grievance mechanisms for migrant farm workers in Spain and Italy | "Widespread distrust of formal grievance systems"; mechanisms poorly communicated and inaccessible |
| Better Work, 41 Cambodian factories (2025) | Workers' understanding of the grievance mechanism rose from **31% to 93%** after training: awareness can be fixed |
| OECD National Contact Points (2023) | 52% of concluded cases reached agreement, but cases took **962 days** on average. An earlier NGO review found remedy for victims in only about 1% of 250 cases (2000–2015). |

**A China example.** In 2002 several brands and a sourcing agent set up a hotline for workers at about 500 Guangdong suppliers that passed complaints on to labour bureaus and legal aid. The companies blocked it because of the negative image it created. A confidential version launched in 2007 handled 1,055 cases in five years and answered within 12 hours, and managers reported lower turnover. The lesson: a channel must be safe enough for workers to use and acceptable enough for factories to keep. *(Hofman, Wu and Liu, Journal of Current Chinese Affairs, 2014.)*

## 2. What a working channel looks like: the eight criteria

The UN Guiding Principles (Principle 31, 2011) set eight effectiveness criteria for non-judicial grievance mechanisms. Every later standard uses them.

| Criterion | In plain words | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Legitimate** | Trusted by the people it is for | Not run by the person complained about; worker representatives involved |
| **Accessible** | Known to everyone it is for, and usable | Several routes, local languages, free, covers agency and dispatch workers |
| **Predictable** | A clear procedure with time limits | Written stages and deadlines that are met |
| **Equitable** | Complainants can get information and advice | Access to a union, legal aid or an adviser |
| **Transparent** | Complainants are kept informed | Acknowledgement, updates and a reasoned outcome |
| **Rights-compatible** | Outcomes respect human rights and the law | Never asks workers to waive legal rights or routes |
| **A source of continuous learning** | Lessons change how things are done | Annual review; trends reported to management |
| **Based on engagement and dialogue** | Designed and reviewed with its users | Workers consulted on design; satisfaction asked after closure |

What makes workers actually use a channel, across the sources: they know it exists, in their own language; there are named trusted people as well as anonymous routes; outcomes are visible; a union or third party is involved; it costs nothing; nobody who used it was punished; and there is one coordinated channel rather than many competing ones.

## 3. What the law asks

| Law | Year | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| **EU CSDDD** Art. 14, as amended by Omnibus I | 2024 / 2026 (applies from July 2029) | A complaints procedure open to affected people and workers' representatives across the chain of activities, plus a notification mechanism for anyone. Complainants can ask for follow-up and to meet the company, and are told the outcome. *The 2026 amendments to this article are still being reviewed for this guide.* |
| **Germany, LkSG** §8 | 2023 | A complaints procedure reaching the company's own operations and its suppliers' workers; impartial, confidential handlers; a published procedure; annual review. *An amendment bill is still before the Bundestag.* |
| **EU Whistleblower Directive** 2019/1937 | 2019 | Internal reporting channels for organisations with 50+ workers; acknowledgement within 7 days; feedback within 3 months; no retaliation |
| **China, Labour Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Law** | 2008 | Negotiate, then mediate, then arbitrate, then sue (Art. 5). Arbitration must be claimed within one year (Art. 27; no limit for unpaid wages while employment continues) and is free (Art. 53). |
| **China, Provisions on Enterprise Labour Dispute Consultation and Mediation** (MOHRSS Order 17) | 2012 | Large and medium enterprises shall set up a mediation committee (Art. 13) with equal worker and management members (Art. 15); cases accepted within 3 working days (Art. 22) and mediated within 15 days (Art. 29); records kept (Art. 32) |
| **China, Trade Union Law** | 2021 (in force 2022) | Unions take part in enterprise dispute mediation (Art. 29) and may demand correction of wage arrears, unsafe conditions and arbitrary overtime (Art. 23). A worker dismissed for union activity is reinstated or paid twice their annual income (Art. 53). |
| **China, Labour Security Supervision Regulations** | 2004 | Anyone may report violations to the labour bureau, which must keep the reporter's identity confidential (Art. 9); violations older than two years are not pursued (Art. 20) |
| **China, Personal Information Protection Law** | 2021 | Notice before collecting complaint data (Art. 17); separate consent before passing it to a buyer (Art. 23); stricter rules for sensitive data such as health (Arts. 28–29); a lawful route for any transfer abroad (Arts. 38–39) |

China has no general whistleblower-protection law. Protection is scattered across the rules above, so a factory's or buyer's channel has to build its own non-retaliation guarantees.

**Codes and programmes.** SA8000 requires a confidential, unbiased, non-retaliatory complaints procedure (clause 9.6 in the 2014 edition). The RBA offers RBA Voices; Fair Wear runs helplines, including in China, and received 139 new grievances in 2023; the FLA takes third-party complaints and in 2025 its investigations recovered USD 300,000 in severance; amfori's Speak for Change is paused pending revision; the Bangladesh Accord has handled over 4,000 safety complaints. SLCP's assessment asks about grievance systems among its worker-involvement questions.

## 4. Across the relationship

| Stage | What the channel must do |
|---|---|
| **Before business** | Check the supplier's channel as part of onboarding (section 6). A factory with no working channel will hide problems you will later be blamed for. |
| **Beginning** | Write the channel into the contract: which channel the supplier's workers can use, how complaints about the buyer's own practices are handled, and how data is protected. See [Responsible Contracting](/en/contract-guide). |
| **In business** | Review the log and trends at each audit; ask workers directly; treat a rise in complaints after a change as a sign the channel is working. |
| **Ending** | Keep the buyer's channel open and advertised to the factory's workers for about **six months** after the last order (ETI 2026). Most problems after an exit (unpaid severance, social insurance) surface late. See [Responsible Exit](/en/exit-guide). |
| **After** | Lessons from complaints feed the next supplier choice and the next contract. |

## 5. A grievance policy in outline

For a factory, a one-page policy covering these points is enough to start. A buyer's version adds how its own channel reaches suppliers' workers.

1. **Purpose and scope:** who can complain (all workers, including dispatch, agency and contract workers, and their representatives), about what (any breach of law, the code of conduct or a person's rights).
2. **Channels:** in person (named people), phone, WeChat or app, a box, and at least one route outside line management; an external option where one exists.
3. **Steps and time limits:** acknowledge within 3 working days; investigate; decide within 15 days where possible; tell the complainant the outcome and reasons; appeal to the mediation committee, union or an external body.
4. **Non-retaliation and confidentiality:** no punishment for complaining in good faith; anonymous complaints accepted; identities protected.
5. **Personal data:** what is collected, who sees it, how long it is kept; separate consent before sharing with a customer; anonymised data only leaves China.
6. **Rights preserved:** using the channel never replaces 12333, labour inspection, arbitration or the courts.
7. **Records and review:** a log of every case; an annual review with worker representatives; results reported to management and, in summary, to workers.
